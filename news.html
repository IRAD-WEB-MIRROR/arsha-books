New books, readings, and what has happened to us since 2009. Newest first.

31 October 2026

The Salt Road

Ormon Satarov’s first western, The Salt Road (1962), is the fourth book of the Ottuk Library in English: twelve camels, a starving valley and the winter passes, with the censor’s cuts put back. Chapter One is free to read now. The ebook follows as soon as the shop abroad has it listed. Read more

20 October 2026

The Rider goes into the archive

The Rider from Kenkol has been taken into the Talas State University Digital Archive (TSU-DA-2026-0148). A suspended press can’t sell a book, but it can still see it kept. Thank you to the library staff who asked us for it.

4 March 2026

Still waiting

Two years ago this month our registration was suspended. The appeal is still pending. We are still here on Saturday mornings, we are still reading your manuscripts, and the free books are still free. If you wrote to us and heard nothing, write again: the post to the Köhne Diz is slow.

16 September 2025

The Widow’s Horse in print, abroad

Readers outside Irad can now order The Widow’s Horse as a print-on-demand paperback through the shop that sells our ebook. We see none of the money until the suspension ends; it is kept for the translator. Read more

11 April 2024

About our appeal

We have lodged our appeal against the suspension with the Department of Public Information. We have been told it will be considered. In the meantime we may not sell or publish books in Irad. Books already deposited with the State Archives stay there, and the ones that are free online stay free, with their authors’ consent.

4 March 2024

Our registration is suspended

This morning we received notice that our registration as a publisher has been suspended for a licensing irregularity. We are not allowed to take orders or print new books until the matter is settled. We do not agree that anything was irregular and we will appeal. Thank you to everyone who came up the stairs today.

2 December 2023

Baylanyshsyz

Forty-two poets, eighty pages: Baylanyshsyz (Without Connection) collects poems written in Talas in October, in the days when the phones went quiet. A few hundred copies, as always. Launch reading on Saturday morning, upstairs, tea provided.

21 June 2023

The Widow’s Horse

Saltanat Kalchakeeva’s The Widow’s Horse (1965), the only Ottuk western written by a woman, is out in English as an ebook. A widow in the Karatau will not give up her husband’s golden stallion, and neither, it turns out, would the censor’s red pencil. Read more

Our first Ottuk Library translation is printed: The Rider from Kenkol by Ormon Satarov, translated by Aidana Mukash kyzy, with every passage the censor cut in 1964 put back in brackets. Legal deposit LD 2022/0861. Read more

27 November 2021

Ak-Boz and the Winter Road

Nazgul’s picture book for children is out: a grey foal too small for the move to the winter pasture goes anyway. Pictures by Dilbar Pak, herding songs at the back. Bring the small ones on Saturday.

Translating the Ottuk Library

Aidana Mukash kyzy has started on the first of the Ottuk westerns. We will publish them in English one by one, with the censor’s cuts put back and marked, so that you can see what the 1960s did not want you to read. Read more

19 October 2019

Forty-one westerns come home

We have bought the whole catalogue of the Ottuk Library, forty-one pocket westerns printed in Iradi between 1962 and 1968, from the liquidators of the old state publisher. A preservation grant from the National Library of Irad paid for it, and we say so plainly, because it is the only money we have ever taken that did not come from readers. Read more

8 June 2019

Ulan Abdyraim uulu’s Toguz is printed: nine stories of horse thieves, the elders who judge them and the ninefold fine, told by a horse.

17 October 2015

Aikokul Tursunbek kyzy’s twelve stories and a coda, from the Bill of Sale to the demarcation, are printed in Iradi. Many of you heard some of them read aloud at the evenings two winters ago.

23 November 2013

Jol Yrlary

Ninety-four horseback songs from the upper Chatkal, recorded between 2008 and 2012 and edited by Kubat Turdakun uulu, with a note on every singer. One hundred and forty-eight pages and our heaviest book yet.

15 September 2012

Igor Lysenko’s poems from a Nurzhan microdistrict, in Russian with Iradi on the facing page. Igor is a fitter at the refinery and wrote most of them on the night shift.

6 February 2010

Kök Tütün

Our first book: thirty-one poems by Nurila Bekbolot kyzy about the Talas valley in winter, sixty-four pages, bound by hand in the back room. We will keep binding until the paper runs out.

1 August 2009

Arsha Books
